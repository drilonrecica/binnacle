# Checks and alerts operations

HTTP/HTTPS checks are outbound requests made by the Binnacle process. Public targets are allowed by default. Private-network targets (RFC 1918, ULA, and carrier-grade NAT `100.64.0.0/10`) are blocked unless the deployment sets `BINNACLE_CHECKS_ALLOW_PRIVATE_TARGETS=true` (`checks.allow_private_targets`, shown in Settings → System and in diagnostics bundles) and restarts Binnacle. Loopback, link-local, multicast, unspecified, `.localhost`, cloud-metadata targets and the reserved ranges `0.0.0.0/8`, `192.0.0.0/24`, `198.18.0.0/15` and `240.0.0.0/4` remain blocked unconditionally. NAT64 and 6to4 addresses are unwrapped and judged by the IPv4 host they reach.

The runner uses the shared outbound-network policy and does not use environment HTTP proxies. It validates DNS results, redirects, and the address actually dialled; follows at most three redirects; reads at most 64 KiB; and stores only a sanitized failure category. Response bodies are never persisted.

Checks are skipped while their resource is paused or archived. The worker count is bounded by `checks.max_concurrency` (default 8). The scheduler queues at most twice that many due checks; when the queue is full the remaining due checks wait for the next one-second tick, and each such skip is counted and logged. A check that is already queued or running is not offered again until its result is saved. The Incidents page opens on open incidents and also lists resolved incidents, firing alerts, the live status of every HTTP check (read-only; checks and rules are managed under Settings › Alerting), and timed silences; notification channels are configured under Settings → Notifications and deliver incident lifecycle events. See [Incidents and notifications](INCIDENTS_AND_NOTIFICATIONS.md).

Container state comes from the Docker container list on every collection tick, so a stopped or exited container is shown as down within one tick even if Docker events are lost. Docker events (stop, die, kill, pause, health changes) update the state between ticks; a health change such as `health_status: unhealthy` takes effect without waiting for the next inspect. When the events stream drops, Binnacle resubscribes with exponential backoff (1 s doubling to 60 s), reports `events disconnected` on the Docker collector meanwhile, and re-inspects every container after reconnecting. Each Docker event is recorded against the resource of its container, which the restart-storm and OOM-loop rules count per resource within their window.

Resolved alerts are retained for one year. Expired silences are retained for 90 days. Alert evaluation continues during a silence or deployment grace period, so a persistent failure fires when suppression ends. Each alert family is evaluated on its own every ten seconds: an error in one family (for example a failed query) is logged and the other families are still evaluated.

## Domain sweep

The domain sweep watches the operator's public domains, independent of Docker resources (ADR 028). Each domain is probed on its own interval, 10 minutes to 24 hours, one hour by default (`sweep.default_interval`, `BINNACLE_SWEEP_DEFAULT_INTERVAL`), and one probe records:

- DNS: the NS set (the domain is registered) and the A/AAAA set (it resolves).
- HTTPS: one `GET https://<domain>/` through the same outbound policy as the checks (private targets blocked unless `checks.allow_private_targets` is set, at most three redirects, 64 KiB of body drained and never kept, a 20-second budget). The final status is recorded as is: a Cloudflare 525 is a status, not a failure code. A request that gets no response records the checks' failure code (`dns`, `timeout`, `connection`, `tls_handshake`, `target_blocked`).
- TLS: the leaf certificate's issuer, expiry and whole days left (negative once expired).
- Headers: whether `Strict-Transport-Security` and `Content-Security-Policy` are on the final response.
- Mail authentication: the apex TXT record starting with `v=spf1`, and the `p=` policy of the `_dmarc.<domain>` record (`none`, `quarantine`, `reject`, or empty when there is none).

Only the latest result per domain is stored. A fingerprint over the status, the certificate's issuer and expiry, the NS and address sets, SPF, the DMARC policy and the two headers is kept with it; when a run's fingerprint differs from the stored one, a `domain_change` event ("example.com changed: HTTP 200 → 525; DMARC policy reject → missing") is written to the Changes page under Health. Days left and timestamps change on their own and are not part of the fingerprint.

Five built-in rules read the latest results every ten seconds, all global and not overridable per domain: **Domain unreachable** (critical: no response or a 5xx), **Domain certificate expiring** (warning, threshold 14 days, editable), **Domain DNS missing** (critical: no NS records or no address), **Domain mail authentication missing** (warning: no SPF or no DMARC policy) and **Domain security headers missing** (warning: no HSTS or no CSP). A value the sweep could not observe holds its rule rather than failing or recovering it: a domain that does not answer does not also fire the headers rule, and an unregistered domain does not also fire the mail rule. Their alerts have target type `domain` and open one incident per domain, so notification channels deliver them unchanged. Removing a domain resolves its alerts.

Domains are managed under Settings › Alerting › Domains (add, pause, run now, remove) or through the API: `GET /api/v1/sweep/domains` lists every domain with its state, `POST /api/v1/sweep/domains` with `{"domain":"example.com"}` (optional `intervalSeconds` 600–86400 and `enabled`) adds one, `GET`, `PATCH` and `DELETE /api/v1/sweep/domains/{id}` read, change and remove one, and `POST /api/v1/sweep/domains/{id}/run` probes it at once. Mutations need a session and its CSRF token, as for checks. The scheduler's worker count is `sweep.max_concurrency` (default 4); `sweep.enabled=false` (`BINNACLE_SWEEP_ENABLED`) keeps the list and the API but stops the scheduler. Demo mode seeds seven synthetic domains that cover every state.

## Time-to-full estimates

Binnacle estimates when each filesystem and host memory will run out. Every five minutes it fits a least-squares line to the used bytes of the last 24 hours (`estimator.window_hours`, 6 to 168, environment `BINNACLE_ESTIMATOR_WINDOW_HOURS`, editable live in Settings) and reports one of:

- `steady`: the line changes usage by less than 1 % of capacity over the window;
- `shrinking`: usage is going down;
- `filling`: usage is going up; `daysToFull` and `fullAt` give when it reaches capacity at that rate;
- `unknown`: there are fewer than three hours of history, or no capacity is known. The interface says "not enough history".

Each estimate carries a `confidence` from 0 to 1: one minus the root-mean-square distance of the history from the line, divided by the larger of the projected change and 1 % of capacity. A clean, steady rate scores near 1; a volume that swings more than it grows scores near 0. Filesystem history comes from the per-mount samples (kept for the raw retention period, 48 hours by default) and memory history from the 1-minute rollups, both averaged into 15-minute buckets. Filesystem capacity is used plus available bytes, so the blocks ext4 reserves for root do not count as free. Estimates are computed in the background and attached to the live snapshot, `/api/v1/server` (`host.memoryTrend` and `filesystems[].trend`) and `/api/v1/filesystems`; a request never triggers a computation.

Every filesystem also has a `role`: `root` (mounted at `/`), `docker` (holds the Docker data root), `data` (holds Binnacle's data directory) or `other`, with that precedence when one disk has several. The Docker data root is inferred from the containers' named volumes, whose host path is `<data-root>/volumes/<name>/_data`. Without a named volume, the default `/var/lib/docker` is assumed. Binnacle does not ask the daemon (`GET /info`), because the socket proxy does not allow that path (ADR 022, ADR 026).

The Headroom page draws one arc gauge per dimension: CPU, memory, then the root filesystem, the Docker data root and the other mounts by fullness, eight instruments at most (further disks collapse into "N more disks"). Memory and disks show the estimate as "full in ~N d", "steady", "shrinking" or "not enough history"; CPU has no estimate and shows "rising", "steady" or "falling" from the last hour of live samples. A gauge's warn and critical lines come from the enabled rules for that target (host CPU and memory rules by severity, `filesystem_warning` and `filesystem_critical` scoped to the mount or global), falling back to CPU 80/90, memory 85/95 and filesystems 80/95; the trend text turns critical when the days to full are under the mount's **Filesystem full within days** threshold (default 7). The page reads the rules when it opens.

The built-in rule **Filesystem full within days** (`filesystem_full_within_days`, warning) fires when a filesystem is filling, would be full in fewer days than its threshold (default 7), and the estimate's confidence is at least 0.6. It must hold for 15 minutes. It recovers when the filesystem stops filling or the projection reaches the recovery threshold (never fewer days than the threshold), and holds while the estimate is unknown or not confident. Like the other filesystem rules, a rule scoped to one mount replaces the global rule for that mount.
